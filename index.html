01 Welcome & Background

Language, literature,
and the classroom

Hello, and welcome. My name is Xiaochen Guo, and I am a first-year Ph.D. student in the Chinese program at Arizona State University. I came to ASU by way of two cities that shaped how I read and how I listen: Nanjing, where I earned my B.A. in Chinese Language and Literature at Nanjing University, and Hong Kong, where I completed my M.A. in the same field at The University of Hong Kong.

My training sits at the meeting point of modern Chinese literature and Chinese linguistics. I have written on the color imagery of Eileen Chang, the spatial poetics of Hong Kong fiction, and the narrative strategies of Mo Yan; I have also surveyed urban language use in Nanjing and traced the ru tone of the Yangzhou dialect across generations of storytelling performances. My articles on Eileen Chang’s color imagery and representations of Yangzhou in Song ci poetry have been accepted for publication in World Chinese Literary Forum and the Journal of Baoding University, respectively.

Language is best learned when it is communicative, culturally meaningful, and centered on the learner.

This semester I teach two sections of CHI 101 — Elementary Chinese I. Working with absolute beginners has confirmed that conviction. My classroom aims to be a place where students gain the confidence to speak, the curiosity to ask why, and a genuine appreciation for the culture the language carries.

Thank you for visiting. I hope these pages offer a clear picture of my development as a teacher, and I welcome your feedback.
